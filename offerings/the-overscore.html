The Overscore

a dance improvisation group-score ritual with live music

Nov 28–29

The Overscore is difficult to describe. It is a journey of collective creative action. An improvised ritual. A performance with no audience~ everyone is in. An abstract prayer. An opportunity to be odd. A fountain of spontaneous magic. A time to be radically present with ourselves and each other. A small shift in the cosmos. A relational dance between chaos and consonance~ between self, other and the whole.

It begins with laying on the floor. Letting go of the day, the week, perhaps your quotidian identity. Sounds slowly start to trickle in from the live musician, a warm harmonic hum envelops the room. Slowly you wake up to the creative beings around you and enter into the first score: a game, creative limitation or spatial orientation that offers a structure to improvise within. A score opens a world. Each world has its own life. It can develop, deepen, and transform.

Somebody stands against the wall. Then another person. Soon half the group has accumulated at the wall, marking the beginning of the next score. The score develops into open improvisation, the seams start to burst. Someone stands at the edge of the space. Then another. Soon the group is in a circle as wide as the room. From chaos, to a moment of stillness. The next chapter is about to begin. So on and so forth. We travel through a structure of scores, a kind of narrative arc, a shared map of checkpoints we feel our way through together.

There’s nothing new under the sun, and the Overscore is no exception. It is a fruiting body of many lineages and teachers.

Nancy Stark Smith

First and foremost the name is of course a nod to Nancy Stark Smith’s work of the Underscore.

Chris Aiken and Angie Hauser

Years ago I took their workshop on improvisational performance at Earthdance, which culminated in a two-hour improvised performance through a series of scores. That long-form structure, and specifically the game sweeps, came to me through them.

Vocal improvisation

Bobby McFerrin, Rhiannon, the Música do Círculo Institute in Brazil, and The Downbeat community here in the East Bay. With The Downbeat we have been exploring what we call a “Portal”, an extended open space where improvised voice, song, movement, text, and theater weave into one ritual arc.
