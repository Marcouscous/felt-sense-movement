The Overscore

an improvised ritual of movement, voice, and live music

November 28–29@ The Finnish HallLive music by Ian Erickson!

The Overscore is difficult to describe. It is a journey of collective creative action. An improvised ritual. A performance with no audience (everyone is in). An abstract prayer. An opportunity to be odd. A time to be radically present with ourselves and each other.

It begins with laying on the floor. Letting go of the day, the week, perhaps your quotidian identity. Sounds trickle in from the live musician, a warm hum envelops the room. Slowly you wake up to the creative beings around you and enter into the first score: a game, creative limitation or spatial orientation that offers a structure to improvise within. Each score opens a world that can develop, deepen, and/or transform.

The Overscore is a series of scores strung together to create a kind of shared map. The map is marked with spatial rendezvous such as people accumulating at a wall, in a circle, or in the corners of the room. In between these spatial checkpoints we journey through different scores and feel our way to the next transition as a group.

There’s nothing new under the sun, and the Overscore is no exception. It is a fruiting body of many lineages and teachers.

Nancy Stark Smith

First and foremost the name is of course a nod to Nancy Stark Smith’s work of the Underscore.

Chris Aiken and Angie Hauser

Years ago I took their workshop on improvisational performance at Earthdance, which culminated in a two-hour improvised performance through a series of scores. That long-form structure, and specifically the game sweeps, came to me through them.

Vocal improvisation

Bobby McFerrin, Rhiannon, the Música do Círculo Institute in Brazil, and The Downbeat community here in the East Bay. With The Downbeat we have been exploring what we call a “Portal”, an extended open space where improvised voice, song, movement, text, and theater weave into one ritual arc.

  1. Is it more voice or movement?

    This iteration is definitely more movement than voice. There is one score that invites some toning over a drone. The container is being held by the live musician the whole time.
